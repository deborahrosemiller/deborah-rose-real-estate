Some of my favorite deals are two transactions stitched together, where the timing of the sale has to line up almost perfectly with the timing of the purchase. That was the case with a pair of transactions I handled for the same clients: a sale in the Waters Edge community on Lake Houston, in Harris County, and a purchase in the Atascocita area of Humble. One had to work for the other to work.

## A forever home

My clients had lived in their Lake Houston home for years, and they wanted to change neighborhoods to be closer to family. This was not a casual move. They had a long list of criteria for what the next house needed to do for their household, and they were clear from our first conversation that this next home was not meant to be a stepping stone. They wanted it to be their forever home.

That changes how you search. You are looking for the house they will not want to leave in fifteen years.

## The sale, and the air conditioner

We started on the sell side, a 2014 two-story with real wood floors downstairs, a large covered porch, a firepit out back and no rear neighbors. The market there was moving, and "moving" does not mean every home sells itself. We priced it from the recent comparable sales, prepared it to show well, and had it under contract at the price point my clients wanted. In a fast market that is not something to take for granted. Plenty of sellers get a quick offer and concede on price to get there. We did not have to make that trade.

Then, partway through, we hit a snag: an air conditioning issue came up during the buyer's inspection period. In a Texas summer that is the kind of thing that can stall or sink a deal if it is not handled quickly and honestly. I worked through the repair with a licensed contractor and negotiated the resolution with the buyer's side so the issue was fixed properly and the timeline stayed intact. The sale closed on schedule in May, at the number my clients wanted.

## The purchase, in a seller's market

While that sale was moving forward, we were hunting for their next home in Eagle Springs, and this is where the market conditions really mattered. It was a heavily seller-dominated market. Buyers were competing hard for well-priced inventory, multiple-offer situations were common, and homes that checked a lot of boxes did not sit.

Buying in that environment while your own sale has to close on a compatible timeline is a hard needle to thread. We found a one-and-a-half story with a porte-cochere, a tandem three-car garage and a game room upstairs that met their long list, moved on it quickly, and negotiated a good price despite the conditions working against buyers generally. The price was amended mid-contract and held.

## Sequencing two closings

Coordinating both sides comes down to sequencing and communication. I was in constant contact with both closing teams, both lenders and both sets of agents, making sure the sale proceeds and the purchase timeline stayed aligned so my clients were never in limbo. No scramble for temporary housing, no rushed decision made out of fear of a gap between closings. The two closings landed about three weeks apart.

Throughout, my clients kept coming back to the same question: what happens if one side falls apart before the other closes? It is a fair question, and it is the one that keeps a lot of sellers from attempting a sell-then-buy move in a fast market. My answer was to build contingencies into both contracts where I could, keep the closing dates close together, and give them a realistic backup plan in case either timeline slipped. Having that plan in place, even though we never used it, is what let them make decisions with confidence instead of anxiety.

## One project, two closings

What made this pair of deals work was treating the sale and the purchase as one coordinated project instead of two separate transactions. When an air conditioning repair threatened the sale, we solved it fast instead of letting it become a bigger negotiation. When the purchase market got competitive, we moved decisively instead of hesitating and losing the house to another buyer. Sell-then-buy transactions live or die on timing, and timing is something you manage.

If you are weighing a move within the greater Houston area, selling in one Harris County neighborhood and buying in another, whether that is Humble, Atascocita or the Lake Houston corridor, I would welcome the chance to help you plan both sides of that move together.
