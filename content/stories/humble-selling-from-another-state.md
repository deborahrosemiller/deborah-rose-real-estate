I still think about this sale because it broke almost every rule of what a hard transaction is supposed to look like, and it still turned into one of the smoothest closings of my career.

## Selling from hundreds of miles away

The sellers were a couple getting ready to downsize, and they were not just moving across town. They were relocating to a different state, which meant that for most of this transaction I was working with clients who were not physically in Humble, Texas at all. There was no popping by the house to grab a forgotten document, no last-minute walk-through together before showings started. We handled nearly the entire sale long distance: phone calls, video walkthroughs, digital signatures, documents crossing state lines by overnight mail.

They were also about to become grandparents for the first time, which put a real date on the calendar.

## A heavy buyer's market

On top of that, we were listing into one of the heaviest buyer's markets I had seen in the Humble area in a long time. Inventory was up, buyers had leverage, and sellers across this pocket of Harris County were sitting on homes for weeks, sometimes months, cutting price after price to draw a nibble of interest. That was the backdrop when we put this home, a four bedroom in Eagle Springs with a pool, on the market.

Here is the thing about this particular couple: they were not nervous first-timers. They had bought and sold many, many homes over the course of their lives. They understood what a normal transaction looked like, they knew what a slow market felt like from experience, and they had realistic expectations about what they might have to compromise on to sell from out of state in a market that was not cooperating.

## Under contract in under a week

So when I tell you we were under contract in less than a week, I want you to understand it in the context of what was happening around us. Homes in their neighborhood were sitting. Buyers were negotiating hard, asking for concessions, taking their time. And yet this listing found a buyer, negotiated terms, and went under contract in under seven days.

It was not luck. It came down to a few specific things. First, pricing the home correctly from day one based on real, current comparable sales in their part of Humble, rather than on what the sellers wished the market still looked like a year or two earlier. Second, professional photography and a listing presentation strong enough to stop a buyer mid-scroll in a flooded inventory of similar homes. Third, being available on their schedule, across a time zone difference, so every question got answered the same day instead of sitting in an inbox overnight.

Long-distance sellers cannot run over and check something themselves, so I had to be their eyes on the ground for everything: confirming a repair had actually been completed, describing how the afternoon light hit the backyard, walking a room with my phone camera so they could see it the way a buyer would.

## Exactly the price they wanted

The part I am proudest of is that they got **exactly the price they wanted**. Not close. Not pretty good considering the market. The number they were asking. In a buyer's market that almost never happens without friction. Usually it means several price reductions along the way, or accepting an offer well under list and calling it a win because at least the house sold. We held firm on a number grounded in real data, buyers respected it because the home was presented and priced honestly from the start, and that number held all the way through to closing in early April.

There was one paperwork snag, a home warranty payment tied to a closing-date discrepancy, and it was resolved before it could matter.

After closing, they told me this was the smoothest transaction they had ever been part of, and the fastest. Coming from people with a long track record of buying and selling real estate, that is not a compliment I take lightly.

## What it demands

The lesson I would pass along to anyone selling in Humble or the surrounding Harris County area, especially from out of state, is this: a tough market does not have to mean a tough sale. What it demands is more precision. Price it right the first time using real comparable sales. Make the online presentation good enough to compete with everything else a buyer is scrolling past that week. And if you will not be in town to handle the small things yourself, make sure whoever represents you is willing to be your hands and eyes on the ground every day until the keys change hands.

If you are weighing a move out of the Humble area, or relocating out of state and need someone who can manage a sale long distance without missing a beat, I would like to hear about your situation and talk through what is realistic for your timeline.
