I met this buyer the way I meet a lot of people, at an open house in the Kingwood area. He was not there for that particular listing. He was there because he was serious about buying in that part of Kingwood and wanted a feel for what was actually available. That first conversation told me everything I needed to know about how to work with him.

## A buyer who knew what he wanted

He was one of the most specific buyers I have worked with. He knew exactly which neighborhood he wanted, and he knew exactly which floor plan he was after. He had no interest in being talked into something close enough. He wanted a particular layout in a particular pocket of Kingwood, and he was willing to wait for it rather than settle.

A lot of buyers say they want to be patient. Very few actually are once a decent house shows up that is almost right. He genuinely was.

## Watching a foreclosure

That patience turned out to be the whole story. I had gotten word that a foreclosure was likely coming onto the market in the exact neighborhood he wanted, with the floor plan he was looking for.

Foreclosures do not move on a predictable schedule. They can sit in process for a long time, the pricing can be unpredictable when they finally list, and there is no guarantee the numbers will ever land where a buyer needs them to. So instead of pushing him toward something else in the meantime, we made a decision together to wait it out and watch this specific property.

Once it listed, we tracked it closely. Banks often start at the price they hope to get rather than the price the market will bear, and a foreclosure can sit for a while before that gap closes. We kept an eye on it as the price was adjusted, waiting for it to drop into the range that worked for my buyer's budget.

That took real discipline. It is easy to jump early because you are worried about losing the property, and just as easy to hesitate too long and lose it to someone else who was watching just as closely. Timing a foreclosure well means knowing the property, knowing the seller's pattern, and being ready to move the moment the number is right.

## Moving fast when it counted

When the price finally dropped into the range he had been waiting for, we did not wait around to think it over. We put together a strong, quick offer and got it in front of the bank fast. There was competition, as there almost always is once a foreclosure hits a price that makes sense. Our offer won the bid.

The transaction closed in late summer, and here is the number that makes this story worth telling: he bought the home with **roughly $100,000 in built-in equity**. That is the real gap between what he paid and what the property was worth in the Kingwood market at the time of closing. It changed his financial position the moment he got the keys.

## What people miss about foreclosure deals

None of that happens without the waiting. If he had gotten impatient three months earlier and bought something in a neighboring part of Kingwood or Harris County just to be done with the search, none of it comes together.

There is also a piece of this that gets overlooked: a foreclosure purchase is not simple just because the price ends up attractive. It comes with its own paperwork, its own timelines and its own risks. The property still needs to be evaluated honestly. Financing still needs to be lined up and ready to move fast. The offer still needs to be structured so the bank takes it seriously against other bids. Part of what made this work was having everything prepared in advance, so that when the price hit the number we were waiting for, we were executing a plan that had already been thought through.

## The lesson

Knowing exactly what you want is a strategic advantage, as long as you pair it with real patience and someone who is tracking the specific opportunities that match your criteria. Foreclosures and other distressed sales reward buyers who know precisely what they are looking for and have someone watching closely enough to move the second the timing is right.

If you have a specific neighborhood or floor plan in mind in the Kingwood area and you are willing to be patient for the right opportunity, I would like to help you watch for it. Sometimes the best deal is the one that has not listed yet.
