Sometimes the fastest way to get a client into the home they want is not to negotiate harder on price. It is to make your own fee part of the negotiation. That is what happened on a sale-and-purchase I handled for clients moving within Porter, in Montgomery County. It is one of the transactions I am proudest of, less because of the price we hit and more because of what I was willing to give up to get there.

## A house search on a clock

My clients had a clear goal. They wanted out of their current home and into a house with a swimming pool, and they wanted it done in time to actually enjoy the summer in it. That detail matters more than it sounds. A pool you close on in October is a pool you look at through a fence for eight months. A pool you close on in the spring is a pool your household gets to use for the whole season. From the start, this was a house search with a date attached.

## Step one: sell the existing home

To buy the house they wanted, they needed their current home in Kingwood sold, and sold for a strong number, fast. We priced it from the most recent comparable sales and put it on the market. It **sold over list price**. In a lot of markets that alone would be the headline. Here it was step one, because the real challenge was still ahead.

## Step two: the bidding war

The house they wanted, a four bedroom in the Azalea District with a heated pool and spa and a three-car garage, was a house other buyers wanted too. Homes with pools that show well and list at the right time of year do not sit around, and this one did not. We found ourselves in a genuine bidding war: multiple offers, buyers competing hard, and a seller working with a large iBuyer who had the luxury of choosing among several strong contracts.

In a bidding war, buyers think about price first. Price is one lever, and it is not always the most persuasive one to a seller who already has several strong offers. I made a decision in that negotiation that I think mattered as much as anything else we did: I adjusted my own professional fee to make my clients' offer more competitive. It was not a dramatic gesture, and it was not something I did lightly. It was a real, tangible way to strengthen their position without asking them to stretch further than they were comfortable with.

That adjustment, combined with a clean, well-structured offer and a fast, credible close, won the bid. Between the quick sale of their old home and the win on the new one, the whole process moved fast enough that they closed in June, with real time left in the summer, at their existing home with a mobile notary.

## The part they may not have noticed

I ran both sides of this deal, the sale and the purchase, while recovering from a broken foot, getting around Porter on a knee scooter the entire time. Showings, negotiations, the walk-through before closing, all of it happened one-legged and a little slower than usual. It did not change the outcome. It did mean some creative maneuvering just to get from the car to the front door, and it is the kind of detail that reminds me clients deserve full effort no matter what is going on behind the scenes.

## On commission

I think about this deal whenever I hear agents talk about their commission as a fixed line item that stays off the table no matter what. I understand the instinct. My job is to get my clients into the home they actually want, on the timeline that actually matters to them. In this case, being willing to flex on my own compensation was the tool that got the job done, and I would make the same call again.

## On timing

The other lesson is about starting early. My clients did not wait until June to start looking for a pool home for the summer. They started early enough that we had room to sell their existing home properly, search for the right replacement, and survive a competitive bidding situation without running out of runway. Start that search in May instead, and the whole equation changes: less room for a bidding war, less room for a fast close, more pressure to settle for whatever is available.

If you are planning a move in Porter or elsewhere in Montgomery County and you have a seasonal deadline in mind, the biggest favor you can do yourself is to start early. And when you are competing for a home you really want, remember that price is one variable among several. I would be glad to talk through what levers might be available to you.
