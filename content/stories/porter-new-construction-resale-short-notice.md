Not every listing starts with excitement about a fresh start. Sometimes it starts with a phone call that is really about health. That was the case with a couple I worked with in Porter, in Montgomery County, who needed to sell their home because a medical situation changed their plans almost overnight. When a health issue forces a sale, the emotional weight is different from a typical move, and I try to carry as much of the logistical burden as I can so my clients can focus on what matters.

## The complication

This was a nearly new home, built in 2022 in the Royal Brook section of Porter, and they had not owned it very long before circumstances required them to sell. Selling new construction shortly after purchase comes with a set of challenges most sellers do not anticipate.

There is very little built-up equity to work with when you have owned a home for a short period, which means the numbers have to be managed carefully so the seller does not walk away from the closing table owing money. A new-construction resale also competes directly with the builder, who may still be selling similar floor plans in the same community, sometimes with incentives like closing cost credits or rate buydowns that a resale cannot match dollar for dollar.

## Making the numbers work

Given all of that, my first priority was the arithmetic. I went through their original purchase price, what they had put into the home since closing, current builder pricing and incentives in the same community, and recent comparable sales, to find exactly where we needed to land to get them out whole.

Price a home like this too aggressively and you risk a loss. Price it too high in a market where the builder is still actively selling and it sits, and the pressure on the sellers only builds. We landed on a number that reflected the true value of the home while staying competitive enough to move quickly, which was the priority given their circumstances.

## Faster than expected

It worked faster than I expected. We found a buyer quickly, which was a relief for everyone involved. A fast offer does not mean a simple transaction, though. Because the home was so new, there were details specific to recently built properties that needed attention: builder warranty transfers, and making sure any remaining punch-list items or paperwork were accounted for so nothing came back to bite either side after closing.

I stayed in close contact with the buyer's agent throughout to keep expectations aligned and avoid surprises. Closing happened right after a storm came through, so we confirmed there was no damage before proceeding. Then the buyers and the sellers signed together, the same day, at the property.

## The result

The sellers **came out even**. Given the short ownership window and the circumstances driving the sale, that was exactly the outcome we were working toward from day one. Both sides were happy with how it went, and the transaction closed smoothly despite the unusual pressure on the front end. For my clients, it meant one less thing to manage during a hard stretch of life. For the buyers, it meant a like-new home in a growing part of Montgomery County without building from the ground up.

## When life forces a move

I think about this transaction whenever someone tells me they are worried that a health situation or another life event means they made a bad decision by buying and then having to sell sooner than planned. That is rarely true if it is handled correctly. This couple had not made a mistake buying new construction. Their circumstances changed faster than anyone could have predicted, and the job in front of us was to adapt.

The broader lesson is about pricing a new-construction resale with real precision. Comparing against what the builder is actively offering, understanding exactly what has gone into the home since purchase, and being clear about the limited equity window are what protect a seller in this specific situation from taking a loss they do not need to take. When life forces a move, the goal is to protect my clients financially while getting them through the process with as little added stress as possible.

If you are facing a sale driven by health, family or any other circumstance that is forcing your hand, especially in a newer home with limited equity, I would be glad to walk through your options with you. These situations require a different kind of care, and I take that seriously.
