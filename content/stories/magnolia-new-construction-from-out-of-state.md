One of the more satisfying deals I have closed involved no open house at all. My buyer never set foot in the home before we wrote the offer. He was relocating for a new job through his employer's relocation program, this was going to be his very first home purchase, and he was doing all of it from out of state while wrapping up a life somewhere else and starting a new one in the Magnolia area of Montgomery County.

## Three challenges at once

First-time buyer, relocation, and new construction is its own particular combination. Most first-time buyers want to walk through a house two or three times before they commit. Most relocation clients want to see the neighborhood in person, drive the commute themselves, get a feel for the traffic on a Tuesday morning. My client had none of that. He had a start date, a budget and a list of priorities, and he needed someone on the ground in Magnolia who could be his eyes, ears and advocate.

We started with the criteria. He wanted new construction: no deferred maintenance, no surprises, a home that was genuinely his from day one. He needed something that made sense for his commute, which narrowed the search to specific pockets of Montgomery County where the highway access worked in his favor. And he had a short list of must-haves inside the home: layout, garage space, a floor plan that would function for how he actually lives.

We found a one story Highland Homes plan in the NorthGrove community off FM 1488 that checked every box. Right location for the commute, right builder, right price point.

## New construction still gets an inspection

Buying new construction sight unseen is different from buying a resale sight unseen. With a resale, you rely on disclosures and an inspection to tell you what is wrong with a house that has already lived a life. With new construction, you rely on the builder's quality control, and builder quality control varies far more than most buyers assume, especially on a home built on a schedule.

Because he could not be there, I decided early that this deal would run through an inspection the same way it would for any resale, maybe more carefully. New construction buyers sometimes skip this step, assuming a brand-new home does not need one. I do not operate that way, and I told him so up front. We scheduled a full inspection before closing, and I was physically present for it, walking the house room by room with the inspector so I could report back to my client in real time. Photos and notes the same day, room by room, so he could see exactly what I was seeing.

That inspection turned up a handful of items that needed the builder's attention before we signed off. Nothing catastrophic, but the kind of punch-list issues that are common on new builds and that absolutely should be caught before a buyer takes possession. Because we caught them early and documented them clearly, the builder addressed every item without a fight. My client got a home that met the standard he was paying for.

## Below market

The other piece of this deal I was proud of: we negotiated below market. That is not always possible with new construction, where builders tend to hold firm on price and lean on incentives instead. Because we had done our homework on comparable sales in the area and were patient about timing, we got my client into this home for less than similar new builds in the neighborhood were commanding. For a first-time buyer starting a new chapter in a new city, that meant a lower monthly payment from day one and equity built in before he had unpacked his first box.

Since the move came through a corporate relocation company, part of my commission went to that company, which is how those arrangements work. It did not change what my client got.

By the time he flew in for closing in July, the house was ready, the punch-list items were resolved, and the only thing left was to sign and get the keys.

## Distance is a reason to raise your standards

When a client cannot be there to see something for themselves, my job is to go look, in person, and tell them exactly what I see, the good and the not-yet-good, so they can make the same informed decision they would make standing in the driveway. New construction in particular deserves that scrutiny, because "new" does not automatically mean "done right."

If you are relocating to Montgomery County, considering new construction in the Magnolia area, or just want someone who will treat an inspection as seriously as you would if you were standing there yourself, I would like to help. Whether you are across town or across the country, I will be your eyes on the ground.
