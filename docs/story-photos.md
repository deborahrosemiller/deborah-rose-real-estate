# Story photos: what is in use, where it came from, and what is missing

Prepared 2026-09-18 for the story-by-story pass. Every story, the photo in use, its source folder in the Property Archive (Google Drive, "Deborah Rose Real Estate Website / Deborah Rose Real Estate - Property Archive"), the source file's native pixel size, and whether the property match is confirmed against the HAR notes in that folder and the Interview Prep Brief.

## The finding that explains every photo complaint

**The archive holds HAR gallery thumbnails, not photographs.** Most folders contain files 296 to 800 pixels wide. Three folders (Shady Maple, Windward Falls, Boothill Rd) contain screenshots of a photo viewer saved as PNG. One folder (Lombard Wood) holds the builder's representative photos, not the home. The first build resized everything to 1600 pixels wide, which is a two to five times upscale; that is the fuzz Brett and Deborah saw on the cards.

As of this pass nothing is upscaled. Each kept photo is exported at its native size, front exteriors are used wherever the folder has one, and the screenshot and wrong-house photos are removed (those cards show the city as type until a real file arrives). At card size on a Retina screen a photo needs about 800 pixels; at article hero size, about 1,800. **Only one source in the archive reaches 800.** So the practical answer for every story is the same: a full resolution exterior from Deborah's photographer or from HAR's full-size gallery.

Rights note for the deep dive: on the buyer-side stories (Lockridge Harbor, Regal Landing, Maple Park, Blackstone River, Ehlers Rd, the Porter downsizing story, Lombard Wood, Shady Maple) the listing photos belong to the listing brokerage or its photographer, not to Deborah. Her own listings are hers to use. Worth a decision before launch.

## The May 28 card, for Deborah to settle

On the call Deborah said the May 28 photo was the wrong house ("Royal Brook doesn't have a pool"), and that the story is a foreclosure in a heavy buyer's market.

What the records say: the May 28 story is item 2 in her Interview Prep Brief, 3330 Lockridge Harbor Lane, Royal Brook at Kingwood: "Repeat client (Deborah had recently sold their previous home). Searching for a dream home in a specific neighborhood with a pool and upgrades, within budget. Closed in under a month in a heavy seller's market." The HAR record for that address lists a pool and spa, 5,057 square feet, $779,000 list, $767,000 sold, October 2021, buyer represented by Deborah. The original article ("Finding the Dream Home in a Seller's Market") says pool, upgrades, seller's market, repeat clients. The photo on the card was that house's HAR aerial, so the photo matched the story as written.

The foreclosure in a buyer's market is the September 3 card (5511 Regal Landing Dr, brief item 10), which has its own story and photo.

So one of two things is true. Either the May 28 story is right and she was looking at the wrong card, or the interview notes for item 2 are wrong and the Lockridge Harbor story needs retelling. The story was left as it is. She decides on the deep dive. The other Royal Brook purchase in the archive, 3318 Opal Stone Court (September 2021, $575,000, no pool in its photos), was skipped in the interview and has no story.

## The selection rule going forward (Brett, 2026-09-18)

Default to a hero exterior of the property. Never a screenshot. Never upscale; check native pixel size first and reject anything that will render soft at card size (800 px) or article hero size (1,800 px). Interior or feature shots only when the story is about that feature, and only at full resolution.
