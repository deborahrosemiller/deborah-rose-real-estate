# Area pages: generic or local, and the questions that turn one into the other

Prepared 2026-09-18 for part two of the review. Brett's test: the thing that makes Google want to recommend her is any key insight about these places that a reader could not get by googling the town. Every paragraph on the five area pages is marked below.

**GENERIC** means a reader could get it from a search result or an encyclopedia. **LOCAL** means it reflects working knowledge only someone selling there would have. For each generic paragraph there is the question Deborah would need to answer to replace it. Ask the questions, record her answers in her words, and the rewrite writes itself, the same way the case studies did.

Where the copy lives: `src/content/areas.ts` (intro, three or two body paragraphs, neighborhood list, and the closing first-person paragraph for each town).

Reading the score: 5 pages, 19 paragraphs. 13 generic, 6 local. The local ones are nearly all her biography, not the place. Almost nothing on these pages tells a buyer something about the town that only she knows.

## Fair housing, before any of this is written

Every answer describes the place: flood maps, drive times, dues, feeder boundaries, lot sizes, tax rates, what a dollar buys. None of it describes who lives there or who would fit. School districts are named as fact and never rated. That is the line the case studies hold and these pages will hold it too.
